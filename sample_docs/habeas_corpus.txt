Habeas corpus: challenging unlawful detention

Habeas corpus is a court order that requires the government to bring a detained person before a judge and justify the detention. The phrase is Latin for "you shall have the body". It is one of the oldest protections against unlawful imprisonment.

The United States Constitution provides that the privilege of the writ shall not be suspended except when public safety requires it in cases of rebellion or invasion.

A petition for habeas corpus does not ask whether the prisoner is guilty. It asks whether the detention is lawful. Typical grounds include a conviction obtained in violation of the constitutional right to counsel, a sentence longer than the law allows, or detention without any charge.

A person held under a state conviction must usually exhaust the remedies available in state court before a federal court will hear a habeas petition. Federal law also sets a one-year filing deadline in most cases and limits second or successive petitions.

If the court finds the detention unlawful it can order the prisoner released, order a new trial, or order a new sentencing hearing.
